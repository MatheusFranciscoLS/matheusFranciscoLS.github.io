Vardão Central

The management system that Vardão Máquinas, an authorized Husqvarna dealer in Limeira, Brazil, uses every day at the counter, in sales, finance and inventory. It started as a parts catalog for the counter and now takes over parts of the store’s old ERP, starting with finance.

  • RoleSolo development: requirements, business rules, architecture, implementation, testing and maintenance
  • CompanyVardão Máquinas, where I work
  • PeriodBuilt since August 2026
  • StatusIn daily use in the store’s operation

The problem

At the counter, the customer is standing in front of the salesperson. To answer "which part is it and how much", you had to open a PDF catalog, find the position in the drawing, check the price in a spreadsheet, see whether the part was on the shelf and write the quote by hand. A wrong part number means a return.

Away from the counter, sales, finance and customers lived in an old ERP, separate from everything else. Knowing how much the store sold in the month or which customer had gone quiet meant putting together information from different places.

Responsibilities

I built Vardão Central on my own, from requirements to daily use in the store:

  • Requirements and business rules. I gathered the needs with the people who use it, in the store itself, and turned them into rules, flows and tests.
  • Architecture and data. I designed the architecture (front end, API, queue and database) and modeled the data in PostgreSQL with Prisma.
  • Full-stack implementation. React and TypeScript front end and a Node.js and Express API, with the counter, sales, finance and inventory modules.
  • Integrations. The manufacturer’s public portal, PDF catalog reading through a queue, AI restricted to a closed list of parts and the import of the old ERP’s data.
  • Testing, deployment and maintenance. I wrote the test suites, set up CI and deployment (Vercel, Render and Supabase) and keep evolving and fixing the system in use.

Features

Counter

Search by part number, name, model or PNC; the official exploded view with clickable positions; list price and shelf; quotes as PDF or via WhatsApp. Search and quoting work from the keyboard (Ctrl K, arrows, Enter and +), because service at the counter is fast.

Sales

A CRM with a deal board and filters, customer ranking, cross-selling, reactivation of idle customers and the machine price list.

Finance

Recording of sales, purchases and expenses, income statement, cash flow, working capital, ABC analysis and cash closing.

Inventory

Items to order, shortages, shelves and special part orders for customers.

Role-based home

Each person opens the system on the home screen of their role, with what matters for their day, and the main action changes with the role: "Record sale" or "New quote".

Business rules

Rules the system guarantees, all covered by automated tests:

Validated part numbers

A typed part number returns only the exact part and the manufacturer’s official replacement. The AI only chooses within a closed list of parts for the machine.

Internal data stays internal

PDF quotes and WhatsApp messages carry only what the customer needs to see; the counter’s internal data stays out.

Dates in the store’s time zone

The server runs in UTC and the store in UTC-3. Every day calculation uses the store’s time zone, so "today" includes the day’s own quotes.

Amounts in cents

In sales, amounts are whole cents and splitting into installments always adds up to the total, with no rounding difference.

Replacing the old ERP

The switch could not lose a single number: the same results as the old ERP, or better.

  • Rules read from the ERP’s code. Instead of trusting memory of how the ERP worked, I read its code screen by screen and reproduced the rules: income statement, cash flow, working capital, customer ranking and others, each one with tests.
  • Undocumented rules. Reading the code revealed rules and history from older versions that were not documented. Without it, that legacy would have been left out of the reports.
  • Controlled import. The importer runs first as a dry run, only writes when it receives the expected totals, checks the totals in the database after writing and can be undone. It was tested with fictional data and used to import the store’s real data.

Architecture

  • Separation by store. The structure supports more than one store in the same database: 44 of the 49 models record the store that owns each record. The other five are the store itself, items that inherit the store from their parent record (parts of a catalog, items of a quote) and the manufacturer’s shared official catalog. The store comes from the signed session, validated on the server, not from a value sent by the browser. Today the system serves one store.
  • Role-based access. Login with a signed session and role permissions, always checked on the server, not just in the interface.
  • Basic protection. Rate limiting, security headers and input validation in the API.
  • Background work. PDF catalog reading goes through a RabbitMQ queue, with retries. The counter does not depend on the queue to work.
  • Database. PostgreSQL with Prisma: 49 models and 91 versioned migrations.

Technical decisions

A passing test did not guarantee it ran in production

A maintenance routine had a passing test, but in production it never ran: it was wired in the wrong place of the startup. I found it by looking at the data. Now a specific test covers that wiring, validated by undoing the fix and watching the test fail.

Refusing a doubtful catalog

The PDF catalog reader refuses a doubtful file and explains why, instead of saving a wrong part number that would compromise the search.

Latency between the API and the database

The API server was far from the database, and every query paid the round trip. I prepared the everyday screens to be served by a layer closer to the database, leaving only the heavy work on the main server. The change can be switched off with an environment variable.

How the numbers were obtained. Run locally on 10 Nov 2026, in an isolated environment (a disposable local PostgreSQL and fictional data), on the repository state of 4 Nov 2026. These are not CI results. On the back end, all 1,491 passed with a real RabbitMQ 3.13 in the same environment. In the end-to-end suite, 2 of the 70 tests used a fixed date and failed on the first run; the fix (the current month’s date, without removing or weakening checks) was merged, and the suite passed 70 of 70.
